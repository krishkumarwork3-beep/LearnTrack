# LearnTrack — User Guide (How to Use It at Maximum Efficiency)

## 1. First-time setup, each semester

**Step 1 — Enter your weekly timetable (once).**
Add every recurring weekly commitment: classes, labs, breakfast, lunch, dinner, sleep, anything else fixed.

**Step 2 — Review computed free time and set your study targets.**
The app shows your free hours per weekday; you decide how much of that you actually want to study.

**Step 3 — Add exams, then topics under each.**
For **every** exam — normal quiz or MST/EST — enter its **credits and marks weightage upfront**, since those describe the evaluation itself and don't depend on knowing a date.

- **Normal quiz/evaluation** — also enter the exact date, start/end time, and travel time to the venue, all upfront.
- **MST/EST (major exam)** — if the datesheet isn't out yet, just enter a rough window (earliest possible start) instead of a date. You don't need start/end time or travel time yet — those only become relevant once the real date is known. **Until the datesheet arrives, the app treats your window's start date as if it were the real exam date** and schedules those topics into your normal weekly routine right alongside everything else — nothing special happens on that date itself, it's just used to judge urgency, the same as any other deadline.

Add topics per exam: importance, difficulty, learning hours, revision hours per cycle, and how often to revisit it.

**Step 4 — Add your skills** — set up once, ever, unaffected by semester changes.

---

## 2. Your daily routine

1. Open the app → **Dashboard** → today's suggested timetable, built from your real free time.
2. Edit it if you like — swap or resize sessions.
3. Log each session honestly:
   - **Learning a topic for the first time:** finished → Yes; not finished → tell the app how many more hours you think you need.
   - **Revising a topic:** fully completed → Yes, and it's freshly revised, ready to come due again after your set interval. Not finished → Not yet with a new estimate — the app keeps it urgent rather than treating it as freshly revised.
   - **A skill session:** just log that you practiced it, whatever the length — no hours estimate needed.
   - **Solving PYQs:** works exactly like a topic — finished → Yes; still have more to go → Not yet with a new estimate.
4. **Any of this — finishing early, falling short, a class getting cancelled — regenerates your entire remaining semester calendar**, not just today or tomorrow.

---

## 3. If a class or lab gets cancelled

Just mark it cancelled for that date. The freed time is absorbed into the same whole-calendar regeneration — it reflows to wherever it's needed, automatically.

---

## 4. How PYQ practice works (same rule everywhere, no special day)

There's no fixed "reserve 2 hours the day before" rule. Instead: **once you've fully covered every topic under an exam that's still in scope**, the app starts working 2 hours of previous-year-question practice into your schedule for that exam, fitted in wherever it naturally fits as the exam approaches. If you haven't finished the syllabus yet, PYQ practice simply won't show up — covering the topics always comes first.

---

## 5. Normal quizzes and evaluations

**Two exams the same day:** split by **credits × marks weightage** — a high-mark test in a lower-credit subject gets more time than a low-mark quiz in a higher-credit subject.

**Exam day:** you can keep studying right up until you actually need to leave for the venue — no wasted buffer beforehand. Once the exam ends, a 1-hour buffer is kept clear before the next thing gets scheduled, still working around your meals and other fixed times.

**The day before:** the majority (80% by default) of your free time that day goes to revising that exam's topics (and its PYQ practice, once eligible), and the rest to whatever else is most urgent. **If two exams both happen to fall the day after** (so today is the eve for both), that 80% is itself split between them by the same credits × marks weighting used for same-day clashes — the higher-stakes exam gets the bigger share of the revision time, rather than one exam taking it all.

---

## 6. MST / EST season (major exams)

All the MST exams in a semester count as one "batch," and all the EST exams count as another — they're planned and negotiated together as a unit.

**Before the datesheet:** nothing special happens — those topics are simply part of your normal Dashboard, scored as if the exam were on your entered window's start date, and scheduled around your regular weekly timetable like everything else. Credits and marks weightage are still in effect from setup even at this stage. **If the datesheet is delayed past even your rough window**, the app doesn't break or drop the subject from your plan — it just keeps treating it as increasingly urgent (falling back to your window's end date, and then to "due immediately" if that passes too) until a real date finally arrives.

**Once the datesheet is released for a subject:** enter its real date, start/end time, and travel time. From that moment, that specific exam switches into exam-mode: your weekly class timetable stops applying to exam-window days for that batch, and instead the app has you set up a simple exam-period routine (meals, sleep, other fixed times) that applies across the whole window. This switch happens independently per subject — if only some datesheets in the batch are out, only those subjects switch over; the rest keep running normally until their own date arrives.

**On the day of a dated exam:** study right up until you need to leave, resume 1 hour after it ends, meals and other fixed times always respected.

**If everything doesn't fit** in the time you actually have once dated:
- The app asks if you can add more study hours per day.
- If not, it asks you to mark your most important topics — the rest are set aside and stop competing for time until the window closes.
- This repeats until the plan genuinely fits.
- **Once the exam window ends**, any topics set aside during this trimming automatically become eligible again.

---

## 7. If you miss a day entirely

Open the app the next day — it detects the miss and regenerates the whole remaining calendar automatically.

---

## 8. Starting a new semester

Starting a new semester **permanently deletes** the old semester's exams, topics, and timetable — you'll be asked to confirm first. **Your Skills tab is completely unaffected** and keeps tracking across every semester. Even after all your exams for a term are done, the app keeps generating a rolling schedule (checked every time you open the app) so your skills don't go unattended in the gap before your next semester.

---

## 9. Rules of thumb

- **Log every session honestly** — every log triggers a full recalculation of your whole remaining term.
- **Enter credits and marks weightage for every exam upfront**, even MST/EST ones you don't have a date for yet — it's what the app uses to weigh same-day clashes and PYQ urgency correctly.
- **Don't worry about entering MST/EST exam times early** — just the rough window is enough until the datesheet actually exists.
- **Mark cancellations as soon as you know** — the sooner it's marked, the more the rest of your semester benefits.
- **Update the real date the moment a datesheet is released** — that's what switches the exam into its proper exam-mode planning.
- **Trust the PYQ timing** — it only shows up once you've genuinely finished the syllabus for that exam, so if you don't see it yet, that's a sign there's still topic work to do.
- **Treat "not enough time" prompts as useful signals**, not failures.
- **Before starting a new semester, be sure you're done with the old one** — deletion is permanent (skills are the only thing that always survives).
